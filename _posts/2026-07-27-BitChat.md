What if you could chat with people nearby without internet, cell service, phone numbers, or accounts of any kind — and then seamlessly bridge those conversations to the global internet when connectivity returns? That's the vision behind **BitChat**, a decentralized peer-to-peer messaging app from permissionlesstech that's been rocketing up the GitHub trending charts. Built natively in Swift for iOS and macOS, BitChat combines local Bluetooth mesh networking with the Nostr protocol to create a messaging experience that feels like IRC but works in a world far more hostile to privacy.

BitChat's architecture rests on a dual-transport design that is genuinely novel. The **Bluetooth mesh layer** handles offline, local communication — devices discover each other automatically over Bluetooth LE, forming ad-hoc multi-hop relay networks that can span up to seven hops. Messages route through nearby peers without any internet connection, using the Noise Protocol for end-to-end encryption with forward secrecy for live sessions. It is compact, binary, and optimized for Bluetooth's tight bandwidth constraints. When internet is available, BitChat falls back to **Nostr relays**, using a custom private-envelope format inside kind-1059 events to send encrypted messages across a global network of 290+ distributed relays. Stars: 30,268 ⭐ (+thousands today).

The feature set reads like a privacy advocate's wishlist: **location-based channels** using geohash coordinates, **IRC-style slash commands** (/slap, /msg, /who), intelligent message routing that automatically selects the best transport, LZ4 compression for efficiency, adaptive battery modes, and an emergency triple-tap wipe that clears all local data instantly. Cryptographic identities are ephemeral — a fresh key is generated per geohash area — and there are no persistent accounts, no phone numbers, and no central servers. The project has attracted 4,700+ forks and over 1,000 commits from both human contributors and Claude-assisted development.

Why is BitChat trending now? In an era of increasing network surveillance, platform censorship, and centralized control over communication infrastructure, the appetite for truly permissionless messaging has never been stronger. BitChat doesn't just promise privacy — it works in the hardest scenario imaginable: completely offline, in disaster situations, protests, or anywhere networks are unavailable. The project explicitly acknowledges it has faced takedown demands, which only underscores its relevance. Whether you're a privacy advocate, a protocol nerd fascinated by hybrid mesh architectures, or just someone who misses the raw simplicity of IRC, BitChat is worth watching.
