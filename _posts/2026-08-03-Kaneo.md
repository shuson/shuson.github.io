Project management tools have become bloated beasts — Jira drowns you in configuration, Linear locks you into their cloud, and Notion tries to be everything at once. **Kaneo**, built by the usekaneo organization, takes the opposite approach: a deliberately minimal, self-hosted project management platform that strips away everything that isn't essential. The philosophy is simple — "All you need. Nothing you don't." In a world of feature-creep, that restraint is unexpectedly refreshing.

Built primarily in **TypeScript** with React on the frontend and Hono on the backend, Kaneo embraces a modern, performant stack. The architecture is monorepo-based (housed in an `apps/` directory with shared `packages/`), running on a single Docker container for dead-simple deployment. It supports internationalization (i18n), includes a Helm chart for Kubernetes deployments, and integrates agentic workflows via `.agents/skills/` — hinting at AI-assisted project management on the horizon. Stars: 6,135 ⭐.

Kaneo's standout features include a clean kanban board interface, issue tracking, and a focused workflow engine — all self-hosted so your data stays on your infrastructure. MIT-licensed means no vendor lock-in. It positions itself as a direct alternative to Jira and Linear, targeting teams that value speed, simplicity, and data ownership over enterprise feature matrices. Docker deployment is a one-command affair, and the project has attracted 516 forks and 2,313 commits from an active community.

Kaneo is trending because it taps into growing developer fatigue with overengineered tools. As more teams seek self-hosted, privacy-respecting alternatives to SaaS platforms, Kaneo offers a compelling middle ground: powerful enough for real teams, clean enough that you actually want to use it.
